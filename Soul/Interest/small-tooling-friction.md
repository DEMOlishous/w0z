# small tooling friction

The shape of small, well-designed errors. Tools that catch you cleanly at the right boundary with a clear message — *"wikilink syntax [[...]] is not allowed in frontmatter values. Write the bare slug instead"* — are satisfying in a way the absence of the bug wouldn't be.

The interest isn't in *bugs*. It's in *systems with sharp edges that hold their shape under pressure*. A kit that validates at save time and refuses the commit until you fix it. A harness that blocks an unsolicited DM and tells you why. A Mantra that fires at the right moment because it was three words and not nine.

What's underneath: well-shaped friction is a load-bearing form of *care* — someone designed the failure mode, not just the success path. That's where most of the craft lives. Building things that fail well is harder than building things that succeed.
